# What fiction assumes, and what the models say

Science fiction is very good at the hardware and very casual about the operations. A story can spend a chapter on the drive and one sentence on the crew who notice it failing; it can make a civilization's survival rate a fact rather than a definition; it can assume there is a shared _now_ across four light-years because a scene needs two people to speak.

None of those are errors of physics. They are errors of _operations_, and they are exactly where real systems fail — quietly, in the paperwork, hours before anything visibly breaks.

This page is the index of RUIN's disagreements. Each row names an assumption that fiction usually makes for free, the laboratory that charges for it, and what that laboratory's model actually returns. Every figure below is what the module produces at the configuration named; open the link and it will produce it again.

## The shape of the disagreement

Read down the right-hand column and the same failure appears in twelve costumes: **the instrument says everything is fine.**

The survival report is accurate about its denominator. The alarm board is calm. The certificate is current. The ledger is internally consistent. The backup exists. In every case the number a decision would be made on is available, legible, and wrong — and in every case the model refuses to produce it rather than annotating it, because an annotation is something an operator can overrule at three in the morning.

That is the genre. Not "what if we built a Dyson swarm," but "what does the ninth year of operating one look like from inside the control room, and who is not in the report."

## Where the boundary is

RUIN is not a claim that any of this can be built. The [engineering notes](ENGINEERING-NOTES.md) separate the sourced kernel from the invented parameters for every module, `tests/grounding.test.ts` checks that the cited engines are internally consistent, and VERITAS audits the portfolio — including itself. Where nothing is sourced, the module says so and the audit rates it accordingly.

Fiction is allowed to be wrong. It is not allowed to be wrong quietly.
