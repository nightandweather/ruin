# RELIQUARY — century-scale knowledge preservation

A hundred-year project's quietest enemy is entropy in three currencies: media decay corrupts bits, format death leaves intact bits unreadable, and institutional forgetting leaves readable archives nobody knows how to restore. RELIQUARY couples all three so an operator can watch a "safe" archive die of the one budget they neglected.

## Executable contract

A hundred-year iteration couples per-copy media decay (half-life by medium class) against scrub cadence — the archive loses data only when every counted copy fails inside the same scrub window; format readability, where migration cadence must outpace reader extinction; and institutional memory, which decays exponentially and is renewed only by staffed restore rehearsal. Readability and restore knowledge gate survival exactly like intact bits: an archive nobody can read has not survived.

Incidents: **reader extinction** halves the remaining format lifetime overnight; **curator loss** removes three stewards at once.

**Safety invariant — verified restore, or it doesn't count.** A backup that has not been restored in rehearsal counts as zero copies, whatever the media. Unverified redundancy is a rumor, not a plan.

## Sourced baseline and speculation

- Media classes genuinely decay on different timescales: magnetic tape is rated in decades, archival film and fused-silica storage in centuries.
- Format obsolescence and reader extinction are documented archival failure modes, and digital-preservation practice (LOCKSS, the 3-2-1 rule) is built on independent copies plus scheduled verification.
- The specific half-lives, the knowledge half-life, and the probability composition are RUIN scenario coefficients, not measured reliability data.
