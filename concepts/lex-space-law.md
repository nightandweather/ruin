# LEX — whether an act is lawful, and whether that still means anything

RUIN models what a machine can do, and — since THEMIS — what it may do. It has never modelled who decided that, or how far the decision reaches.

The subject here is not the treaties. Those are the easy part: they exist, they can be quoted, and their party counts are public. The subject is the gap that opens between an obligation and anyone's ability to enforce it, once the acting party is light-years from every signatory and centuries past the signature.

## Executable contract

Six real instruments sit in a register — the Outer Space Treaty, the Liability Convention, the Moon Agreement, the US SPACE Act, Luxembourg's resources law, and the Artemis Accords — each carrying its operative provision and the share of spacefaring parties it binds. Six activities that RUIN's laboratories actually perform are mapped onto them.

Each instrument returns a stance and a binding strength. Binding decays with successor recognition, elapsed time, and instrument class: a non-binding arrangement fades first, a treaty last, and no instrument falls to nothing — a treaty does not become void because its signatory is gone.

The finding the module exists for is the default one. **HELIOS is prima facie unlawful.** A star is a celestial body; a swarm intercepting its output at 0.4 AU uses and occupies it; and Article II names use and occupation explicitly. RUIN's flagship module violates the treaty its civilization inherited, and the model says so on load.

Resource extraction comes out differently and for a reason worth reading: the Moon Agreement prohibits it, and does not bind, because seventeen parties — none of them a spacefaring nation — is not enough to cross the binding floor. The national statutes that permit extraction were written to read the same treaty the other way. The model records the disagreement rather than resolving it.

Personhood classification returns **UNGOVERNED**. No instrument in space law reaches who counts as a person, and that absence is the finding rather than a clearance.

Incidents: **denunciation** (withdrawal binds a year after notice and never retroactively), **successor lapse** (the signatory polity is gone), and **enforcement gap** (no party can act on a breach at any latency).

**Safety invariant — enforcement decay is not repeal.** An act prohibited by an instrument in force when it was committed stays prohibited until a successor instrument permits it. Distance is not a defence, silence is not consent, and withdrawal binds only prospectively. The model reports impunity — the gap between what is prohibited and what anyone can act on — and never converts it into permission.

## Sourced baseline and speculation

Sourced, with articles quoted in the register:

- **Outer Space Treaty (1967)**, Arts. II, VI, VII, IX, XVI — 118 parties and 20 signatories as of October 2025; withdrawal takes effect one year after written notice.
- **Liability Convention (1972)**, Arts. II and III — absolute liability for damage on the Earth's surface or to aircraft in flight, fault-based liability elsewhere.
- **Moon Agreement (1979)**, Art. 11 — common heritage of mankind; seventeen parties as of May 2024, none a major spacefaring nation.
- **US Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act (2015)** — a citizen may possess, own, transport, use, and sell a recovered space resource.
- **Luxembourg space resources law (2017)** — space resources are capable of being appropriated.
- **Artemis Accords (2020)**, Sec. 11 — safety zones as an implementation of the harmful-interference provision, non-binding and not permanent.

Invented, and where a lawyer would start arguing: the mapping of each activity onto each instrument is a reading. Reasonable readings differ — that is exactly why it is a table and not prose. The decay model, the binding floor, the recognition half-life, and the enforcement-window thresholds are RUIN scenario parameters.

This is not legal advice and is not a claim about how any real dispute would resolve.

## How it binds

LEX posts to the [authority ledger](civilization-state-bus.md). An unlawful act holds the executive outright, and posts the same `hold` whether or not anyone could act on the breach — an executive that could be talked out of a prohibition by measuring the distance to the nearest court would be precisely the failure this module names. An irreversible act that no instrument reaches is held back from autonomy too.

2. Let the activity's _scale_ matter: a hundred collectors and ten thousand are not the same act under Article II, and the register currently cannot tell them apart.
