# CONCILIUM — who can afford the infrastructure, and who decides about it

THEMIS's council has always been a number: nine nodes, five reachable, quorum met. Nodes that never wanted different things. CONCILIUM gives the council members worlds, and gives the worlds economies — because a council's power is its jurisdiction times its money, and RUIN had never priced either.

## The currency is energy

A dollar figure for a Dyson swarm is fake precision of exactly the kind VERITAS exists to catch: there is no price series to extrapolate from and no market that has ever cleared one. Terawatt-years of delivered energy is a unit this repository actually computes, so systems are priced in what it costs to build and run them, and worlds are rich in what they generate and sell.

Six resources trade against that numéraire. Licensed designs carry the highest margin and ship as bits; bulk manufactures carry the lowest and the largest volume. That single asymmetry is why twelve thousand people on a research station out-earn nine billion on Terra.

Ownership is not a budget question alone. A system costs capital, draws upkeep, requires prerequisite systems, and consumes resource inputs. Three separate things can stop a world:

- **Capital.** Proxima cannot afford a foundry.
- **Upkeep.** Ceres can pay for one and cannot power it — 1,874 TW·yr of savings against 9 TW of generation. Money is not the same resource as watts.
- **Access.** Proxima is not priced out of the market, it is _distanced_ out of it: no neighbour is close enough to ship from inside the trade window.

Three resources have a single supplier. Rare metals come from Ceres alone, and under an embargo the world that owns the entire swarm drops from nine systems to three. Fifty thousand miners hold the richest world's portfolio at their discretion, and no budget substitutes for the material.

## The council

Seats are drawn by revenue, by population, or by holdings — and the toggle is the whole politics. On the same proposal, with the same worlds and the same evidence:

- **by revenue** — carried, 95.4% for
- **by population** — failed, 0.0% for
- **by holdings** — failed, 32.1% for

The representation gap measures how far the seats are from the people: zero by construction under a population basis, 98.9 points under revenue, where a station of 240,000 holds 95% of the council.

**Safety invariant — dependency without exit is not consent.** A world that relies on a system it cannot build, and that could not reach the vote in time, has not agreed to anything. The proposal is refused rather than carried over its silence, however large the majority among the worlds that could answer. Rushing the vote window turns a 98.6% landslide into `CONSENT NOT ESTABLISHED`.

It is the same rule CENSUS applies to an excluded cohort and LEX applies to an unreachable signatory, and it is the third time this repository has arrived at it from a different direction.

## Sourced baseline and speculation

Nothing here is sourced. Every figure — population, production, price, capital cost, upkeep, trade window — is a RUIN scenario parameter chosen to make a structure legible.

What is defensible is the structure: that capital, upkeep, dependency, and distance together produce an ownership pattern no world chose; that a single-supplier resource is political power regardless of its price; and that seats and people are not the same shape unless someone decides they should be.
